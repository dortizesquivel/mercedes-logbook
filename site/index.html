Mercedes Trips
Install Privacy Docs GitHub

Every drive, logged by Home Assistant.

Mercedes Trips records each trip of your Mercedes electric or plug-in hybrid: when and where it started and ended, how far you went, the energy it used and the route. It keeps them in a local database and shows them on a map in your dashboard.

Open in HACS Read the docs

It reads your car through mbapi2020, so set that up first.

What each trip records

It watches your car's odometer, GPS and state of charge, and turns their changes into trips like this one.

Start and end. A trip starts when the odometer moves and ends after 8 minutes without it changing, so a stop at the shops splits a drive in two. You can change the timeout.

Distance is the odometer at the end minus at the start. Trips shorter than 0.5 km are dropped as noise.

Energy is the drop in state of charge times your battery's usable capacity, which you enter once. Consumption follows from it.

Route. A GPS point every 30 seconds while the car moves.

Addresses for both ends come from OpenStreetMap's Nominatim, looked up once per place.

It reads your car through mbapi2020

Mercedes Trips doesn't connect to Mercedes itself. mbapi2020 links Home Assistant to your Mercedes me account and shows the car's live state; Mercedes Trips turns the changes in that state into a logbook.

You need

  • Home Assistant 2026.3 or newer, with HACS.
  • mbapi2020, listed in HACS as MercedesME 2020, signed in to your Mercedes me account.
  • A Mercedes electric car or plug-in hybrid with an active Mercedes me connect subscription.

In your dashboard

  • Every trip on the map

    Each in its own colour, on OpenStreetMap. Open one and the others step back.

  • Quick periods

    Today, yesterday, 7 days, this month, last month or this year. Or your own dates and hours, like only the trips that started after 18:00.

  • Compared with the period before

    Distance, trips, energy and average consumption against the same number of days just before. Lower consumption shows in green.

  • A logbook by day

    Start and end time and street, km and kWh. Open a trip for the full addresses, duration, consumption, battery used and odometer.

  • Seven sensors

    Distance this month and this year, energy this month, average consumption, total trips, the last trip and the trip in progress, for your automations.

  • Your data, queryable

    A local SQLite database and a small REST API, for your own reports.

Install it

  1. 1

    Set up mbapi2020

    In HACS, search for MercedesME 2020, download it and restart Home Assistant. Then add the MercedesME 2020 integration in Settings → Devices & services and sign in with your Mercedes me account.

  2. 2

    Download Mercedes Trips

    In HACS, search for Mercedes Trips, download it and restart Home Assistant. Or open it in HACS straight away.

  3. 3

    Add the integration

    In Settings → Devices & services, choose Add integration and search for Mercedes Trips. Your car's entities are already filled in. Enter your battery's usable capacity: 66.5 kWh on an EQA or EQB, for example; the README has a table.

  4. 4

    Add the card to a dashboard

    Edit a dashboard, choose Add card and search for Mercedes Trips, or paste this:

    type: custom:mercedes-trips-card

    Your next drive is the first trip in the logbook.

What leaves your Home Assistant

Your trips stay at home, in /config/mercedes_trips.db, which the recorder never purges. Two things go out.

  • The start and end coordinates of each trip, to OpenStreetMap's Nominatim, to look up the addresses. Once per place: after that the address comes from a local cache. No API key and no account.
  • Requests for map tiles to OpenStreetMap, when someone opens the card.

Nothing else: no telemetry, no account and no CDN. Leaflet and the card's font ship with the integration. Anyone who can sign in to your Home Assistant can open the logbook, so it is as private as your Home Assistant is.

Open and checked

MIT licensed, with the source, the tests and the release workflow in the open.

  • Every change runs CodeQL, the Python and browser tests, and the HACS and Home Assistant validation before it can merge.
  • Every release is built by GitHub Actions with a signed build-provenance attestation, so you can check where your copy came from. SECURITY.md explains how.
  • The assurance case sets out what the integration must protect, where untrusted data comes in and how each risk is handled.
  • Found a security problem? Report it privately.
